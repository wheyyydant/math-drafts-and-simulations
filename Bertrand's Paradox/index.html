Bertrand's Paradox: Mathematical Formalism

A study in geometric probability, measure-theoretic ambiguity, and maximum entropy invariances.

1. The Core Threshold Inequality

In the unit disk D², an inscribed equilateral triangle has side length s = √3. For any chord at distance d ∈ [0, 1] from the origin, its length is L = 2√(1 - d²). The condition L > √3 simplifies to:

L > √3 ⇔ 2√(1 - d²) > √3 ⇔ 1 - d² > 3/4 ⇔ d < 1/2

2. The Three Induced Push-Forward Measures

Method 1: Random Endpoints (P1 = 1/3)

θ1, θ2 ∼ 𝒰[0, 2π) ⇒ Δ ∼ 𝒰[0, π],   d = cos(Δ/2)
fD(1)(d) = 2 / (π√(1 - d²)) ⇒ P1(d < 1/2) = ∫01/2 2/(π√(1 - d²)) dd = 1/3

Method 2: Random Radius & Jaynes Invariance (P2 = 1/2)

d ∼ 𝒰[0, 1],   φ ∼ 𝒰[0, 2π)
fD(2)(d) = 1 ⇒ P2(d < 1/2) = ∫01/2 1 dd = 1/2

Method 3: Random Midpoint Area Measure (P3 = 1/4)

M ∼ 𝒰(D²) ⇒ FD(3)(d) = πd²/π = d²,   fD(3)(d) = 2d
P3(d < 1/2) = ∫01/2 2d dd = [d²]01/2 = 1/4

3. Monte Carlo Asymptotic Bounds

By the Central Limit Theorem, the empirical estimator converges with 95% confidence bounds:

&Pcirc;N = (1/N) ∑ 1{di < 1/2},   Error Margin (95% CI) ≈ ± 1.96 √(p(1-p)/N)
