Expected Logarithmic Growth Rate: $g(f) = \mathbb{E}\left[\ln\left(\frac{W_{t+1}}{W_t}\right)\right] = p \ln(1 + bf) + (1-p) \ln(1 - f)$

The 4 Regimes of the Growth Curve

  1. Under-betting ($0 < f < f^*$): Growth is positive but suboptimal; lower volatility and lower drawdown.
  2. Optimal Kelly ($f = f^*$): Maximum asymptotic log-wealth compound rate $g(f^*)$.
  3. Over-betting ($f^* < f < f_c$): Volatility drag reduces growth rate to levels attainable with much less risk.
  4. Ruin Zone ($f \ge f_c$): Expected growth rate $g(f) \le 0$. Bankruptcy occurs with probability 1 as $T \to \infty$.

Volatility Drag Approximation

For small edges and fractions, Taylor expansion yields:
$$g(f) \approx f \cdot \mu - \frac{1}{2} f^2 \sigma^2$$ where $\mu = pb - q$ and $\sigma^2 = p(1-p)(b+1)^2$. The quadratic penalty $\frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$ represents the exact thermodynamic cost of variance.

The Paradox & 3 Resolutions

Paradox: Payoff is $2^k$ with probability $1/2^k$. Arithmetic expected value: $$\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2^k} 2^k = 1 + 1 + 1 + \dots = \infty$$ Yet virtually nobody will pay even \$30 to play!
1. Log Utility (Bernoulli 1738): Rational agents maximize $\mathbb{E}[\ln(W_0 - C + X)] = \ln(W_0)$. For $W_0 = \$1,000$, fair price $C^* \approx \$10.95$.
2. Finite Casino Bankroll: Capped at $2^M$ implies $\mathbb{E}[X] = 1 + M/2$. (If cap is \$1M, $M=20 \implies \mathbb{E}[X] = \$11$).
3. Ergodic Time Average: Repeated trials with fixed ticket price $C > C^*$ lead to guaranteed bankruptcy ($W_t \to 0$).

The Non-Ergodic Illusion

When a system is non-ergodic, the Ensemble Average $\langle W(t) \rangle = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N W_i(t)$ does NOT equal the Time Average $\overline{W} = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \int W(t) dt$.

In multiplicative betting with $f > f_c$:
  • Ensemble Mean: $\mathbb{E}[W_T] \to \infty$ (dragged upward by a single billionaire outlier).
  • Individual Median: $\text{Median}(W_T) \to 0$ (99.9% of players are completely wiped out).
